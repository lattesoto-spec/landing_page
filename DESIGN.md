# CareMin design direction

The marketing site should feel specific to CareMin and the work of Australian aged-care provider
teams. These rules keep future pages consistent with the current direction.

## Product truth first

- Prefer genuine CareMin screens with demonstration data over reconstructed or invented interfaces.
- Use a screenshot only when it supports the adjacent claim. Give it a functional caption.
- Keep essential meaning in page text rather than relying on text embedded inside screenshots.

## Visual restraint

- Use warm paper tones for the opening canvas, dark ink for high-contrast sections, and teal only as
  a controlled product/interaction accent.
- Do not use gradient blobs, decorative grid backgrounds, or floating shapes as filler.
- Do not italicise fragments inside headings or paragraphs for emphasis.
- Do not add an eyebrow, badge, or category tag above every heading. A label needs a real navigation
  or status purpose.
- Reserve rounded containers for interfaces, forms, and functional controls. Use spacing, rules, and
  type hierarchy for ordinary content.

## Layout and copy

- Give each section one clear visual priority; supporting items should be quieter.
- Avoid repeating centred headline plus three equal cards as the default section pattern.
- Write concrete product behaviour and user outcomes. Avoid vague claims that could describe any
  software product.
- Keep necessary regulatory qualifications in the FAQ or legal pages so they do not interrupt the
  main product story.
